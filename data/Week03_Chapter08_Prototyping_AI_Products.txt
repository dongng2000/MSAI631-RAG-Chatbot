Week 3 – Chapter 8: Prototyping AI Products (Master Guide)
1. Core Idea: What Is a Prototype?
Definition

A prototype is:

Any artifact that turns an idea into something users can experience and give feedback on

Key Insight
It does NOT have to be complex
It can be:
Sketch
Mockup
Slideshow
Role-play
Basic working system

 Prototype early, not perfectly

2. Why Prototyping Matters (Very Important)

AI systems are:

Expensive
Time-consuming
Risky
Benefits of Prototyping
Test ideas quickly
Save time and money
Get early feedback
Align teams
Identify what NOT to build

 Key takeaway:
Prototyping reduces risk before full development

3. Unique Challenges in AI Prototyping

AI is harder to prototype than regular software because:

Personalization depends on real data
AI outputs are uncertain
Explainability is difficult
Full ML systems are expensive to build

 This is why low-cost testing methods are critical

4. What Should You Test in AI Prototypes?

You should define the goal first.

4 Key Objectives:
1. Desirability
Do users want this?
Will they use or pay for it?
2. Usability
Can users use it easily?
3. Explainability
Do users understand AI decisions?
4. Relevance
Are AI outputs useful and accurate?

 These 4 = core evaluation framework

5. Types of AI Prototypes
A. Desirability Prototypes

Test:

Interest
Value
Methods:
Fake ads
Popups
Surveys
Mockups

 Also called:
“Smoke test” or “Painted door test”

B. Usability Prototypes

Test:

Interaction
User experience
Fidelity Levels
Low Fidelity
Sketch
Wireframe
High Fidelity
Functional system
Key Goal:

Can users complete tasks successfully?

6. Special AI Prototyping Techniques
1. Using Personal Data
Users bring real data (photos, contacts, etc.)
Simulate realistic outputs

 More accurate than fake data

2. Wizard of Oz Method (VERY IMPORTANT)

Users think:
 They are interacting with AI

Reality:
 A human is behind the system

Why It’s Powerful
No need to build AI
Tests real interaction
Reveals user expectations
3. Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

A simple working version used to:

Test assumptions
Learn quickly

 Important:
MVP is NOT the final product

7. Testing Methods for Prototypes
Task Completion
Can users finish tasks?
Measure time and success
Fly-on-the-Wall
Observe users silently
Identify confusion and errors
8. Explainability in AI Prototyping

AI must explain:

Why it made a decision
Goal:
Increase trust
Improve decision-making
How to Test Explainability
Internal Testing
Team evaluates clarity
User Testing
Interviews
Surveys
Task-based testing

 Key insight:
Good explanations improve trust

9. Relevance Testing

Check:

Are AI outputs useful?
Methods:
Show results to users
Collect feedback
Compare expectations

 Important:
Even accurate AI can feel useless if not relevant

10. Hardware Prototyping (Microcontrollers)

This connects to your assignment (VERY IMPORTANT):

Examples:

Arduino
Raspberry Pi
Smart devices
Challenge:
Hardware is expensive and slow
Solution:
Simulate in 3D (Unity, AR)
Then build physical prototype
