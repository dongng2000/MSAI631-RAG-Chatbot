Week 5 Lecture Summary

This lecture focuses on Large Language Models (LLMs), ethical considerations, training data preparation, privacy concerns, synthetic data, Constitutional AI, and model distillation. It connects AI technology with human-centered design by examining how AI systems can be developed responsibly, transparently, and ethically.

Part 1: What Are Large Language Models (LLMs)?

Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced AI systems designed to understand and generate human-like text. They are built using transformer architectures and trained on massive datasets containing books, articles, websites, and other text sources. LLMs can perform tasks such as:

Question answering
Language translation
Summarization
Content generation
Conversational interactions

Examples include ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama.

Part 2: Ethical Challenges of LLMs

The lecture emphasizes that while LLMs are powerful, they create significant ethical concerns.

Bias and Fairness

LLMs learn from human-generated data, which may contain biases and stereotypes.

Potential problems:

Unfair recommendations
Discriminatory outputs
Reinforcement of societal biases

Developers must actively detect and reduce bias during training and deployment.

Misinformation and Hallucinations

LLMs sometimes generate information that appears correct but is actually false.

This phenomenon is called:

Hallucination

Examples:

Fake citations
Incorrect facts
Fabricated events

This is particularly dangerous in:

Healthcare
Law
Finance

where accuracy is critical.

Privacy Concerns

Training data may contain personal or sensitive information.

Risks include:

Exposure of personal information
Reproduction of confidential content
Violation of privacy regulations

Developers should use:

Data anonymization
Privacy-preserving techniques
Secure data handling practices

Accountability and Transparency

A major challenge is determining responsibility when AI produces harmful content.

Important questions include:

Who is responsible?
How was the decision made?
Can the output be explained?

The lecture stresses the need for accountability frameworks and greater transparency.

Part 3: Preparing Big Data for AI Training

Training successful AI models requires high-quality data.

Data Conversion

Before training, data must be transformed into machine-readable formats.

Examples:

Tokenization
Encoding
Preprocessing

These steps convert raw text into numerical representations that AI models can process.

Data Augmentation

Data augmentation expands datasets through modifications.

Examples:

Text
Paraphrasing
Back translation
Synonym replacement
Images
Rotation
Flipping
Scaling

Benefits:

More training examples
Better model generalization
Reduced overfitting

Crowdsourcing

Crowdsourcing collects large amounts of human-generated data.

Examples:

Human labeling
Sentiment annotation
Classification tasks

This helps improve training quality and dataset diversity.

One-Shot Learning

One-shot learning allows models to learn from very few examples.

Applications:

Rare languages
Specialized medical diagnoses
Personalized AI systems

Benefits:

Reduced training costs
Faster adaptation

Part 4: Synthetic Data

Synthetic data is generated by AI rather than collected directly from humans.

Why Use Synthetic Data?

Challenges with traditional data:

Limited high-quality data
Copyright concerns
Privacy restrictions

Synthetic data offers:

Unlimited scalability
Lower dependence on scraped internet data

Challenges of Synthetic Data
Bias Amplification

Synthetic data may inherit flaws from original datasets.

Model Collapse

Training AI on AI-generated content repeatedly may reduce quality over time.

Copyright Questions

Even synthetic outputs may be influenced by copyrighted material.

These concerns make synthetic data an active area of research.

Part 5: Intellectual Property and Copyright

The lecture discusses major legal concerns regarding AI training.

Copyright Issues

Questions include:

Can copyrighted content be used for training?
Does AI-generated content infringe copyrights?
Who owns AI-generated outputs?

These issues remain legally unresolved in many jurisdictions.

Fair Use

Some countries allow limited use of copyrighted material under fair-use doctrines.

Factors include:

Purpose of use
Amount used
Market impact

However, AI training remains a subject of ongoing legal debate.

Part 6: Privacy and Data Protection

The lecture discusses regulations such as:

GDPR

General Data Protection Regulation (European Union)

Key principles:

User consent
Data minimization
Transparency
Privacy protection

Organizations must carefully manage personal data used in AI training.

Part 7: Model Memorization Risks

LLMs sometimes memorize training data instead of learning general patterns.

Risks
Privacy Leakage

Models may expose:

Personal information
Emails
Confidential documents
Membership Inference Attacks

Attackers determine whether specific data was used during training.

Model Inversion Attacks

Attackers attempt to reconstruct original training data.

These issues create significant privacy and security concerns.

Mitigation Strategies
Data anonymization
Differential privacy
Output filtering
Regular audits
Transparency and consent

These techniques help reduce memorization risks.

Part 8: Constitutional AI

One of the most important topics in the lecture.

What Is Constitutional AI?

Constitutional AI aligns AI behavior with human values through predefined ethical principles.

The AI uses a "constitution" to evaluate and improve its own responses.

Goals:

Helpful
Honest
Harmless

How Constitutional AI Works
Step 1: Define Principles

Examples:

Fairness
Safety
Respect
Human rights
Step 2: Self-Evaluation

The AI critiques its own responses.

Step 3: Self-Improvement

The AI revises responses to better align with its principles.

This reduces reliance on constant human feedback.

Benefits
Improved safety
Better ethical alignment
Greater transparency
More trustworthy AI

The lecture cites Claude as a notable example of Constitutional AI.

Part 9: Model Distillation
What Is Model Distillation?

Model distillation transfers knowledge from a large model (teacher) to a smaller model (student).

Process:

Large Teacher Model
          ↓
Knowledge Transfer
          ↓
Smaller Student Model

The student model learns from the teacher's outputs and approximates its performance with fewer computational resources.

Benefits
Faster inference
Lower hardware requirements
Mobile deployment
Reduced costs
Ethical Concerns
Intellectual Property

Can the teacher model legally be distilled?

Privacy

Can sensitive information transfer to the student model?

Bias Propagation

Can the student inherit biases?

Accountability

Who is responsible for errors in the distilled model?

These issues must be carefully managed.
